Positioning

One-line position: a dark ensemble survival fantasy — found family vs. frozen apocalypse — with cultivation/wuxia and progression-fantasy crossover appeal and a horror streak.

Permafrost's commercial identity is the intersection of three reader appetites that rarely meet in one package:

  • Survival fantasy. The ledger-driven, ration-counting apocalypse texture (the strongest hook for trad-adjacent readers; this is the series' most distinctive retail face).
  • Found family ensemble. Five broken strangers becoming Sunforged — the emotional engine, and the thing reviews and word-of-mouth will be about.
  • Crossover power-fantasy worldbuilding. Goetia demon lords, a wuxia nation, a ranked "Ten Great Powers" tablet, dungeon breaks, adventurer guild ranks — the vocabulary of Royal Road / progression fantasy, executed with more literary patience than that market's norm.

The blind prophet POV is the differentiator to lead with everywhere: it's genuinely rare, it's executed at a craft level that earns coverage on its own, and it converts a crowded-market pitch ("frozen apocalypse fantasy") into a singular one ("a blind prophet hiding her god's death from the last man who believes").

Elevator pitch: The sun is gone, the gods are silent, and the kingdom of Kordova is freezing to death under a dome no power can break. Five strangers walk out of a starving village to find answers: a blind prophet whose goddess stopped speaking, the immortal knight who still believes, a runaway child-soldier of a murder cult, a refugee craftsman with a flask and a crossbow, and a widow with souls in her pockets. The cold magnifies your flaws. It does not forgive mistakes.

Target readership

Core: adult fantasy readers 18–40 who live in series

KU power-readers, Royal Road graduates, r/Fantasy and r/ProgressionFantasy regulars. They want long series, ensemble banter, hard-magic mechanics, and are tolerant of darkness if the competence porn delivers. Permafrost is built for them.

Secondary: dark/grimdark ensemble readers

Abercrombie/Lawrence readers who buy on voice and villains. Lead with Dantalion and Elizabeth, the maid scene's moral fallout, and the Book 2 demolition.

Tertiary: disability-rep and craft-first readers

Muse is a marketable rarity: a blind protagonist whose blindness is a fully engineered perception system, never an inspiration prop. This earns organic coverage (blogs, BookTok niches, accessibility-in-SFF conversations) money can't buy. It must be handled as craft, not as a diversity claim.

Who it is NOT for

Cozy-fantasy readers; romance-forward fantasy readers (there is no romance arc on the page); readers who need standalone resolution per volume; readers sensitive to harmed-child backstories and grooming-adjacent content (see Risks).

Publishing path recommendation

Primary recommendation: self-publish into Kindle Unlimited, optionally seeded by Royal Road

The series' genre blend (Goetia + wuxia + western + D&D races) is a feature in the indie/KU ecosystem and a positioning headache in trad. Its chapter rhythm (3,000–3,400 words, cliffhanger-forward), ensemble structure, ranked-powers vocabulary, and series-first economics all match the KU playbook. The craft level is above that market's median, which is exactly where breakout indie series come from. Sequence: finish line edits (Beta Read Pass 1–2 are non-negotiable pre-publication), commission genre-correct covers (frozen palette, character-forward), launch Book 1 and 2 thirty-to-sixty days apart, with Book 3 at least drafted — the Book 2 ending makes a visible Book 3 promise essential.

Trad path, if preferred

Viable but harder: query Book 1 as "dark ensemble survival fantasy, 94k words" (a very saleable length), lead with Muse, comp Jemisin/Wang, and do not mention the wider crossover vocabulary in the query — let it surprise on the page. Expect pressure to make Book 1's ending more self-contained; that note would be correct.

Back-cover blurb — Book One (draft)

Twenty-two days ago, the dome swallowed the sky and the world began to freeze. Twenty-two days ago, the goddess Naika went silent.

Muse is Naika's last prophet — blind since childhood, beloved, trusted. And lying. Every morning she tells Cain, the immortal knight who guarded her through a thousand years of darkness, that their goddess still whispers. Every morning the silence gets heavier.

When a starving village sends them east for help, the road gives them three companions with secrets of their own: a runaway prodigy raised by a cult of killers, a refugee craftsman who can fix everything except himself, and a one-eyed widow carrying the souls of the dead. Ahead lie cities that hoard their warmth, a demon lord with a golden dragon, and something out in the dark that has been following them since the first night — something that knocks.

The cold is getting worse. The food is running out. And the only thing more dangerous than the road to answers is what each of them will do to keep their lies alive.

Query-letter hook (trad path)

PERMAFROST is a 94,000-word adult dark fantasy, first in a planned series, for readers of M.L. Wang's The Sword of Kaigen and N.K. Jemisin's The Fifth Season. A blind prophet must hold a starving party together with a lie — her goddess is dead, and the immortal knight who carries her across the ice is the only believer left. When their road to the silent capital runs through a plague city ruled by an entitled demon lord, the party's youngest member ends the fight by killing a hostage — and the question of what strength owes splits the family they were becoming, just as the thing that has stalked them since the first night begins to knock.

Series promise line (for product pages)

Permafrost is a planned multi-volume series. Book 2 widens the war; Book 3 marches north. Reader advisory: this series plays for keeps.

Risks & mitigations

⚠ This section discusses both books' endings.
RiskSeverityMitigation
Book 2's ending is a retention cliff. The found family — the product readers are buying — is dismantled, a beloved character (probably two) dies, and the final image is a groomed self-surrender to a predator. Some readers will rage-quit at the exact moment you need them to preorder Book 3. High Ship Book 3's first chapters (Gormund's march; any reunion ember) as a sample in Book 2's back matter. Consider ending on the Gormund beat (see Beta Read). Market the demolition honestly to grimdark readers — for them it's a selling point.
Genre-blend confusion. Goetia names, real-world wuxia vocabulary (Mount Hua, Murim, Heavenly Demon), Billy Kidd's Wild-West register, and a Jack London homage share one world. Trad gatekeepers may read it as unfocused; some readers will clock the borrowed names (Ars Goetia, historical outlaws, manhwa stock terms) as derivative. Medium Position for the indie audience that reads this blend as a feature. In copy, lead with the survival/found-family face and let the crossover content be discovered. Long-term: a glossary/lore page converts "kitchen sink" into "wide canon."
Book 1 doesn't resolve. The volume ends mid-blizzard on three cliffhangers; retail readers punish this in reviews when unwarned. Medium Rapid-release scheduling; explicit "Book 1 of an ongoing saga" labeling; the series-promise line above.
Mechanical polish. The dialogue-tag error is visible on page one of a sample. Agents and review-blog gatekeepers will stop reading before the story earns them. High Beta Read Pass 1 before any submission or publication. Non-negotiable.
Title discoverability. "Permafrost" is a real-word title shared by other works (including a well-known SF novella) and is weakly genre-marked alone. Low-Med Strong series subtitle ("Permafrost: Book One of the Sunforged") — which also plants the party name as brand — plus genre-correct cover art to carry the signal.
Sensitive content handling. Carina's arc ends in implied surrender to a sexual predator; her backstory includes child culling. Handled seriously on the page, but marketing must not ambush readers. Medium Content advisories on product pages; never use the Billy ending in promotional copy; trust reviewers to flag it accurately because you flagged it first.

Marketing assets already in the text

The manuscripts are full of extractable promotional material — these are the highest-converting pieces.

  • The knock scene (B1 Ch. 26) — the single best excerpt for ads, samples, and read-along reels. Self-contained dread, no spoilers, ends on "Nobody opens that door."
  • The wish-list campfire scene (B1 Ch. 6) — the found-family promise in one page; ideal for the product-page "Look Inside" window and character-card carousels.
  • Character cards — the five-member party maps perfectly onto the card/carousel format that performs on BookTok/Instagram: Muse ("She counts her steps. She counts her lies."), Cain ("A thousand years of digging. Three things kept."), Carina ("Nobody taught her how to be wrong."), Gormund ("He fixes everything except himself."), Uthesia ("She carries her dead with her. Literally.").
  • Quotable lines — "We don't get to fall because if we fall, nothing gets better." / "Faith without confirmation is just stubbornness wearing better clothes." / "You're the wall, Cain. I'm the blade." / "Because you deserved it the most."
  • The Sunforged name — earned in-text, ironic, brandable: series subtitle, reader-community name ("the Sunforged"), merch line.
  • The villain double-act — Dantalion/Elizabeth dialogue excerpts are shareable gold for the grimdark audience ("I love you." / "I love you too, Dad." / The knife came down.).
  • The Tablet of Ten Great Powers — a ranked-characters list is engagement bait by design (tier-list videos, "who beats whom" threads); publish it as bonus content.
