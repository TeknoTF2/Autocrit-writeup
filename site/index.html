Verdict

Permafrost is a strong, ambitious draft series with professional-grade storytelling instincts and amateur-grade copyediting. The conception — survival horror braided with epic fantasy politics, told through a five-person ensemble anchored by one of the best-rendered blind POVs in the genre — is genuinely distinctive. Setup/payoff discipline is excellent (a bear hide bought in Chapter 7 saves a life in Chapter 13; a weed in a ship's hold decides a standoff between demon lords a book later). The villains are the find of the series: Dantalion and his fourteen-year-old daughter Elizabeth are worth the price of admission alone.

What held the draft back was almost entirely fixable — and most of it has now been fixed. The systemic dialogue-tag punctuation error (1,225 corrections), "hoard" for "horde," the typography drift, the worst repetition fingerprints (the "of a man who…" formula cut from 240 to 67, negation cascades from 28 to 7), and the headline continuity slips (Bloodridge/Bloomridge, the blind-POV break, Cain's eye count, south-for-east) have all been corrected in the revised manuscripts, with every change logged. What remains is the decision-level layer: the timeline reconciliation, the dropped-thread calls, and the structural suggestions. Underneath it all is a writer who measurably leveled up between volumes — and a Book 2 ending bold enough to burn every bridge the series built, which is both its greatest risk and its strongest hook for Book 3.

What the books are

Book One

A starving village sends five strangers east for supplies. The errand becomes a quest west toward the silent capital — through a plague city ruled by a demon lord who hoards a golden dragon, a moral atrocity that fractures the party, and a winter that hunts. It ends mid-blizzard on a triple cliffhanger: a monster outside the cabin door, the party's youngest member riding with charming outlaws, and the man who burned the heroes' entire civilization walking into the dome after them.

Survival Quest Horror Ensemble

Book Two

The survival story braids into an epic: a grief-eating Wendigo, blind surgery aboard a frozen warship, a death and resurrection, a reunion that makes the number five right again — then a model siege arc, the reveal of who caused the dome and why, and a finale that deliberately demolishes the found family it spent two books building. Every member walks away alone. The last image belongs to a predator.
